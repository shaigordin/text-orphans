Closest texts and joins

For any fragment: the traditions, compositions (CTH numbers) and manuscripts it is closest to in all of TLHdig, and for each manuscript a word-by-word check of whether it could be a duplicate or a join.

Traditions

The ten traditions of the paper, defined from the scholarly literature.

Compositions

CTH numbers, each as the average of its manuscripts (not the bins CTH 470, 500, 670, 832, and not the orphans).

    How to read the lists

    Numbers are cosine similarities: 1 means the same words and spellings in the same proportions, 0 nothing in common. Rare words count for more than common ones. Fragments under about 8 words give unstable lists.

    Closest manuscripts, with the duplicate and join checks

    The barcode has one cell per word of the fragment, in line order: the manuscript has the word spelled the same way, spelled differently, not at all. A manuscript passes the checks when it has (a) at least half of the words, (b) every shared word spelled the same way and (c) at least one shared two-word phrase. Duplicates share text, so they pass more often; pieces of one tablet that join usually share little. Click a row to read both texts.
