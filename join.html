Check a join from photos

Put two fragments side by side at their true size, each scaled by the ruler in its own photograph, and compare their line spacing. A visual aid for a proposed join or duplicate, not proof: the photos show neither the break surfaces nor the thickness of the pieces.

How it works

  1. Load a photograph of each piece (with a ruler in it). The photos stay on your computer; nothing is uploaded.
  2. Scale: click the start and end of a stretch of the ruler's black and white 1 cm blocks. The tool counts the blocks; if your ruler has no blocks, type how many cm the two clicks are apart.
  3. Cut out the fragment from the background (choose a dark or light background; drag a box first if the photo shows other objects).
  4. Line spacing: measured automatically, or click the first and last of several lines and give how many line intervals lie between them.
  5. Arrange the two pieces below: drag them, rotate them, align their edges or move one until they touch. Export the picture and the measurements.

Before you trust a fit

  • A photo is flat. A join is decided by the break surfaces, the thickness and the curvature of the tablet, which a photo does not show; matching text and spacing fit a duplicate just as well.
  • An "edge" in the photo may be a break. Aligning edges assumes they are the tablet's edges; check on the tablets or on photographs of the sides.
  • The gap is unknown. Pieces shown touching may in fact be lines apart.
  • Scale is only as good as the ruler: it must lie in the plane of the inscribed surface. Old prints and steep light make the automatic line spacing fail; then count lines by eye and give the range.
  • The cut-out is automatic; dark edges can be clipped. Check the outline against the photo.
